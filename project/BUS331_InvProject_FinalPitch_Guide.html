Phase 4 technical reference

Final Presentation and Committee Defense

Retain the final visual presentation and include a compact, evidence-based investment-committee defense within it.

One recommendation story

The Phase 4 presentation must match the completed Phase 3 workbook: client mandates, 10 securities per client, final weights, Solver comparison, monitoring triggers and stress results. Connect those decisions to the economic review and security research.

Presentation arc

Make the decision easy to follow

  1. Recommendation first: what the committee recommends and why it fits the client.
  2. Mandate and macro evidence: the guardrails and assumptions that control the decision.
  3. Focused portfolio: final holdings and weights, why funds and ETFs are primary, and where any limited individual security earns its place.
  4. Integration trade-offs: overlap or concentration resolved, alternatives rejected, and the Portfolio Manager's final choices.
  5. Risk response: stress result, client consequence, residual risk, corrective action, and hedge or no-hedge conclusion.
  6. Monitoring and implementation: triggers, conditions, reservations, and next actions.

Compact defense

Build targeted questions into the presentation

Reserve a concise defense segment covering all five role questions. Each student answers at least one; on a four-person team, the dual-role member covers both owned roles. Every answer should name the evidence, recommendation, alternative rejected, key trade-off, Phase 3 workbook contribution, limitation, and whether the challenge changes the decision.

  • Client and Macro Strategist: suitability, mandate, macro assumption, and unresolved information gap.
  • Fixed-Income Analyst: fixed-income funds/ETFs, duration, credit, income, liquidity, and rate-risk trade-off.
  • Equity Analyst: equity funds/ETFs, limited individual securities, overlap, idiosyncratic risk, and position size.
  • Portfolio Manager: final selection and weights, overlap/concentration resolution, integrations rejected, and portfolio trade-offs.
  • Risk and Derivatives Analyst: stress evidence, residual risk, and the targeted hedge or supported no-hedge conclusion.

Readiness

Check individual and shared quality

  • The shared portfolio decision is technically consistent across every submitted file.
  • Each student's role-specific evidence is identifiable and peer reviewed.
  • Each Phase 3 workbook contribution includes recommendation(s), alternative(s) rejected, and a key trade-off.
  • The five targeted role questions are rehearsed without memorized scripts or unsupported certainty.
  • Keep licensed research private under course policy. Submit one PowerPoint file for Phase 4; use the Phase 3 workbook as the portfolio evidence.
