Phase 2 technical reference

Security Selection and Hedge Decision

Use this reference with the generated Security Analysis & Selection workflow and the blank selection workbook.

Start with the approved allocation and mandate

Translate each allocation sleeve and client guardrail into a focused candidate set. Funds and ETFs are the primary vehicles. Individual securities are permitted only when they add a distinct benefit, are limited to two or three total, and receive added idiosyncratic-risk and position-size analysis. Individual bonds are not required.

Portfolio boundary

Target 8–10 final holdings

  • Never exceed 10 holdings.
  • Eight holdings can be complete; do not fill a slot merely because it exists.
  • Every holding must have a distinct portfolio purpose and fit the client mandate.
  • Exclude unnecessary duplicate exposure, avoidable concentration, or inappropriate liquidity.

Final-holding evidence

Use one concise scorecard

Exposure and role in the portfolio
Cost and trading expenses
Liquidity
Holdings, sector, and style overlap
Diversification contribution
Key risks
Client-mandate fit

For an individual security, also explain why it is superior to a fund or ETF for that role, its idiosyncratic risk, and the position-size rationale. Compare the strongest realistic alternative and reject it concisely; do not fully analyze every screened name.

Layered ownership

Keep decision rights distinct

  • Fixed-Income Analyst: fixed-income funds and ETFs, duration, credit, income, liquidity, and rate-risk exposure.
  • Equity Analyst: equity funds and ETFs plus any limited individual equities.
  • Portfolio Manager: final selection and weights, overlap and concentration resolution, alternatives rejected, and explicit portfolio trade-offs.
  • Risk and Derivatives Analyst: portfolio-level stress tests, residual risk, and the hedge or no-hedge conclusion.

Derivative boundary

One targeted hedge at most

No hedge is fully valid

A no-hedge conclusion is complete when residual risk is identified and the evidence shows that cost, liquidity, complexity, basis risk, or other trade-offs outweigh the expected benefit.

If one targeted hedge is proposed, document the residual risk, instrument, size, cost and liquidity, trade-offs, risk remaining after the hedge, and the adjustment or removal condition. Do not add a derivative merely to demonstrate a technique.
