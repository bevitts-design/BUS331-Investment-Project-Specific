Phase 2 · Allocation bridge

Capital-market assumptions and portfolio optimization

Use this guide to turn the approved client mandate into a defensible strategic allocation before the committee selects specific holdings. The output is a decision aid, not a completed answer or a substitute for committee judgment.

1. Start with the approved mandate

Copy only the constraints the committee approved in Phase 1: objective, return need, risk capacity, risk tolerance, horizon, liquidity needs, tax considerations, and any prohibited or required exposures. Record the source for every input.

Decision right

The Client and Macro Strategist owns the translation of the client record into macro assumptions and allocation constraints. The Portfolio Manager decides how those inputs enter the integrated portfolio after committee challenge.

2. Build a compact assumption set

For each asset class under consideration, document an expected return, volatility estimate, and the most important relationship to the other asset classes. Use consistent periods and cite current, permissible sources. Explain any adjustment you make to a published assumption.

  • Use enough asset classes to express the mandate without creating false precision.
  • Separate evidence from judgment: show the source, then state the committee’s adjustment.
  • Keep optimistic and adverse assumptions plausible and internally consistent.

3. Test allocation alternatives

Compare a small set of feasible allocations. Each alternative must respect the mandate and show expected return, estimated risk, liquidity implications, concentration, and the main client tradeoff. Reject alternatives concisely; do not manufacture a large optimization exercise merely to appear quantitative.

  1. Define the feasible ranges and any binding constraints.
  2. Run a base case and at least one adverse assumption set.
  3. Compare the result with a simple policy allocation or benchmark.
  4. Record why the selected allocation is preferable for this client.

4. Hand off to security selection

The approved allocation becomes the boundary for the Phase 2 candidate set. Fixed Income owns fixed-income funds and ETFs, duration, credit, income, and rate-risk exposure. Equity owns equity funds and ETFs plus any limited individual-security case. The Portfolio Manager integrates those recommendations, resolves overlap and concentration, and sets final selections and weights. Risk and Derivatives independently tests the resulting portfolio and owns the hedge or no-hedge conclusion.

Required handoff

Submit the selected allocation, key assumptions, rejected allocation alternative, binding constraints, and the explicit tradeoff the committee accepted. Do not begin with ticker symbols and work backward to an allocation.

Quality check

  • Assumptions use consistent periods and traceable sources.
  • Every allocation alternative fits the approved client mandate.
  • The selected allocation has a clear client-specific rationale.
  • Rejected alternatives and the key tradeoff appear in the Analyst Decision Log.
  • The handoff defines boundaries for the candidate set without preselecting holdings.
