Phase 1 · Part 2 · Client Submissions

Client Scenario Analysis and Decision Protocol

Read the scenario. Make a reasoned judgment. Label assumptions. Set the guardrails.

For client-analysis steps

Use the provided case materials

Use the assigned fictional client profile and data to create documented investment criteria before the team screens a bond, mutual fund, ETF, or portfolio allocation. No client interviews are part of this assignment.

1 · Supplied facts

Read your team's three profile slides and the matching client-data rows. Record where each material fact came from.

2 · Reasoned assumptions

For an unanswered IPS field, add a plausible answer based on the scenario and label it as an assumption with its rationale. Mark unsupported exact values as not provided.

Your decision cycle

Five moves from profile to mandate

1

Prepare

Read the assigned client profile and record one tentative, evidence-based interpretation: what the client needs, which constraints matter, and what remains unknown.

Log: The interpretation; supporting case evidence and assumptions; unanswered questions; and the portfolio or security decision it could affect

2

Separate facts and assumptions

For IPS fields the scenario does not specify, write a plausible, labeled assumption with its case-based rationale, or mark the information as not provided when no defensible inference is possible.

Log: The case location for supplied facts; the reasoning and confirmation condition for each material assumption

3

Compare your three clients

Use your team's profile slides and matching data rows to identify how goals, capacity for loss, time horizon, and constraints differ across the three mandates.

Log: Client-by-client facts, assumption labels, conflicts, and a provisional guardrail for each case

4

Challenge and verify

As a committee, test the first interpretation, separate facts from assumptions, and verify material claims in the assigned files, team workbook, or approved source.

Log: Material challenge; source or file location; source date; and Confirmed / Qualified / Contradicted / Not verifiable

5

Set the mandate

State what changed, what remains uncertain, the final judgment, and the guardrail that later security selection or portfolio construction must honor.

Log: Final decision, concise rationale, downstream guardrail, and peer review

Five analyst lenses

Read each scenario through every role

Each role examines the same provided case from its area of responsibility and hands a usable constraint, assumption, or information gap to the committee. All five roles enter a human-first judgment for all three clients before AI use.

Goals and context

Client and Macro Strategist

  • Primary and secondary goals
  • Time horizons and decision dates
  • Income, spending, and liquidity events
  • Tax, legal, values, and other unique constraints
  • Macro conditions that could change the plan
HandoffDraft the client objective, the RRTTLLU facts, and a list of facts still missing.

Income and capital protection

Fixed-Income Analyst

  • Required cash-flow floor
  • Known liability dates
  • Tolerance for price changes when rates move
  • Credit-loss capacity
  • Liquidity and tax treatment
HandoffDraft the provisional maturity, duration, credit-quality, liquidity, and income guardrails.

Growth exposure and equity fit

Equity Analyst

  • Long-term growth need and equity-loss capacity
  • Diversification and concentration limits
  • Sector, style, values, and benchmark restrictions
  • Fee, tax, trading, and liquidity sensitivity
  • Conditions for any limited individual-security exposure
HandoffDraft the screen for equity funds and ETFs and any limited individual equity; do not name a winning product yet.

Integration and trade-offs

Portfolio Manager

  • Required return versus desired return
  • Ability and willingness to bear loss
  • Concentration and correlation risks
  • Priority among competing goals
  • Weight, liquidity, and implementation trade-offs
HandoffReconcile conflicts, identify portfolio-level trade-offs, and specify which selection or weighting decisions require committee approval.

Downside, residual risk, and hedge conditions

Risk and Derivatives Analyst

  • Reaction to a one-year drawdown
  • Client goal most exposed in a bear case
  • Drawdown tripwire and monitoring trigger
  • Residual risks the client can or cannot accept
  • Conditions under which a targeted hedge could be justified or removed
HandoffPropose the drawdown tripwire, name the residual risks to stress, and identify which facts must be verified before any hedge or no-hedge conclusion.

Committee challenge round

Challenge the first interpretation

Use these questions as a committee discussion guide. Do not paste them into an AI tool.

What is the strongest reasonable interpretation that contradicts our initial judgment? Separate case facts from assumptions.

Which scenario detail suggests willingness to take risk but not financial capacity—or the reverse? What assumption would resolve the conflict?

Which claim, number, or product characteristic cannot be accepted until a human checks an approved source?

How could this proposed guardrail fail to protect the client's most important goal in a bear case?

What later bond, mutual-fund/ETF, or allocation decision would change if this assumption is wrong?

Quality gate

Ready for the Phase 1 vote?

  • Each role records at least one human-first judgment for each assigned client before AI use.
  • Every material scenario-based assumption is labeled, tied to a case clue, and kept distinct from a supplied fact or client statement.
  • Every factual claim or number used in the final reasoning has an approved source and as-of date.
  • A missing IPS field receives a reasoned, labeled assumption when the scenario supports one; otherwise it is marked not provided with a confirmation condition.
  • The final reasoning states what changed the judgment and why.
  • Each final judgment creates a testable guardrail for later fixed-income, equity, allocation, stress-test, or hedge/no-hedge decisions.
  • A second team member reviews the evidence trail before the Phase 1 gate.

Stop condition

If a material claim is contradicted or not verifiable, revise the judgment or mark the mandate incomplete. Do not carry uncertainty forward as a fact.

CFA Level I foundation

Use foundational concepts to organize the judgment

Portfolio planning and construction

Understand the client's objectives and constraints before asset allocation and security analysis.

Economics

Connect business-cycle and macroeconomic conditions to the client's decision context without treating forecasts as facts.

Fixed income

Use cash-flow features, yield measures, interest-rate risk, liquidity, and credit analysis to frame later bond decisions.

Pooled investment products

Compare mutual funds and ETFs as implementation vehicles and examine diversification, benchmark, cost, liquidity, and fit.

Risk and professional judgment

Separate risk capacity from willingness, document a reasonable basis, challenge uncertainty, and preserve client-first reasoning.
