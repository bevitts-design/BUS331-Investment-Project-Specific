Investment Committee Simulation

Your four-person investment committee advises three client mandates and manages $500,000 in hypothetical assets. The committee must establish a market view, build and challenge each portfolio, and defend one evidence-linked recommendation.

Simulation premise

Act as the investment committee

Your committee is accountable for three client mandates and $500,000 in hypothetical assets. The work is cumulative: Phase 1 sets the constraints, Phase 2 builds and challenges the portfolios, and Phase 3 issues and defends the recommendation.

Shared accountability

Roles define leadership, not silos. All four members review the full evidence package, vote at every gate, sign the decision record, and prepare to answer questions about any section.

Committee seats

Four roles

Committee seat

Client and Macro Strategist

Own client discovery, the 12-month market view, IPS objectives and constraints, and the handoff from client needs to investment criteria.

Committee seat

Fixed-Income Analyst

Own the income, capital-preservation, maturity, duration, credit-quality, liquidity, and tax questions that shape the fixed-income sleeve.

Committee seat

Fund and ETF Analyst

Own pooled-vehicle due diligence, diversification, benchmark fit, fees, liquidity, tax considerations, and active-versus-passive implementation.

Committee seat

Portfolio Manager and Risk Analyst

Own portfolio integration, risk capacity versus willingness, the drawdown tripwire, decision-log quality, stress testing, and mandate-breach escalation.

Approval path

Three phases

Phase 1

Frame the Mandate

Establish one defensible 12-month market view and convert each assigned client profile into an approved investment mandate.

Approval gateApprove or revise the common market view and all three client mandates. Portfolio construction cannot begin until the vote and action items are recorded.

Required outputs

  • Completed macro starter workbook
  • Completed Analyst Decision Log for all three assigned clients
  • Three completed client IPS documents
  • One-page committee mandate memo
  • Phase 1 decision record

Phase 2

Build & Challenge

Translate the Phase 1 view into capital-market expectations, construct client portfolios, select securities, design risk mitigation, and challenge each recommendation under stress.

Approval gateApprove, revise, or reject each client portfolio. A failed tripwire cannot receive approval without a documented correction.

Required outputs

  • Completed CME workbook
  • Completed bond, mutual-fund, and ETF analysis and selection record
  • Completed integrated allocation, IPS compliance, and stress-test record
  • Phase 2 decision record with client-by-client votes

Defend the Recommendation

Integrate the committee's work into a concise decision package and defend the client recommendations before an investment-committee panel.

Committee operating protocol

Every phase ends with a decision

Analysis becomes committee work only when the team challenges it, votes, and records what happens next.

Pre-read

Circulate the required evidence before the meeting. Every member reviews the full package.

Recommendation

The workstream lead states a clear recommendation and cites the evidence that supports it.

Independent challenge

The Portfolio Manager and Risk Analyst coordinates the strongest counterargument; the analyst whose judgment is challenged must respond with evidence.

Motion and vote

The phase lead states the motion. The Portfolio Manager and Risk Analyst records approve, revise, or reject; all four members vote and ties require revision.

Decision record

Capture evidence reviewed, dissent or reservations, owners, and actions before the next gate.

AI and evidence

Rules of engagement

Permitted

Logic, debugging & challenge

Use AI to explain formulas, test reasoning, generate counterarguments, or surface missing risks.

Verify before use

Summaries, scenarios & estimates

Cross-check every factual claim and number against FactSet, FRED, SEC filings, or the team's workbook.

Not acceptable

Unverified decisions

Do not submit AI-generated allocations, risk metrics, citations, or client recommendations without human verification.

Verification standard

Every AI-assisted factual claim or number must be traceable to a human-verified source. Record the prompt, output summary, verification source, and final human decision.
