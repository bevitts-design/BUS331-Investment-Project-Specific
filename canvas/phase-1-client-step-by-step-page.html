Set up the team record and make your own first reading

Step 1: Save the Part 2 Decision Record

Decision Record: COMMITTEE ROSTER · ANALYST DECISION LOG

Download the student Decision Record and save a team copy as BUS331_[TeamName]_Phase1_DecisionRecord.xlsx. On COMMITTEE ROSTER, enter the team size, each person once in the voting-member list, and an owner for all five roles. Four members may cover five roles by assigning two to one person; that member will cast only one vote. On ANALYST DECISION LOG row 6, enter your three assigned client names exactly as you will use them in the Client column.

Have your submitted Part 1 macro forecast, client scenario data, and your team's profile slides ready. The Client Scenario Analysis and Decision Protocol links to the correct team-specific profile file.

Check: The roster shows four or five distinct voters, all five roles have owners, and the log names the three assigned clients.

Step 2: Record human-first judgments for every role and client

Decision Record: ANALYST DECISION LOG, rows 8–37

Read only your assigned baseline client files. Before using AI, each of the five roles enters at least one provisional judgment for each of the three clients: 15 role-by-client entries in all. Name the client question, the supplied case fact or clearly labeled assumption behind your first view, and your confidence. Use the role's own lane: goals and macro context; cash flow and fixed income; growth and equity fit; portfolio trade-offs; or downside and residual risk. On a four-person team, the dual-role member makes separate entries for both roles.

Keep the initial judgment visible in column F after later discussion. Use the other log fields as the work develops; an early entry does not need to pretend that verification has already happened.

Check: Every role has a genuine starting view for every assigned client before any AI challenge.

Step 3: Read and compare the three assigned scenarios

Team profile slides · Client Scenario Data · Decision Record

Open your team's page in the scenario-analysis protocol, then read its three profile slides and matching rows in the client-data workbook. For each client, list the supplied goals, cash needs, horizon, constraints, target return, standard deviation, and risk classification. Cite the slide or data row. Compare clients before filling the IPS so you do not give three different clients the same generic mandate.

For a missing client fact, write “Not provided.” You do not need to invent expenses, account balances, tax rates, contribution room, or family details. Explain only gaps that could materially change the mandate. Label and justify any essential modeling assumption from case clues. Identify return and risk policies, allocation weights, and review rules as committee proposals rather than client facts.

Check: Your working notes separate supplied facts, reasoned assumptions, and information gaps for each client.

After reading the scenarios

Challenge the interpretation and write the mandates

Step 4: Map each client's objectives and RRTTLLU

Client files · IPS, Sections 1–3

For each client, rank the goals and document Risk, Return, Time horizon, Tax, Liquidity, Legal, and Unique circumstances. Separate ability to bear loss from willingness to take risk. Note conflicts and missing facts, especially when an attractive return target clashes with a near-term cash need or loss limit. The target return and standard deviation in the client data are case inputs to assess, not automatic committee-approved promises or risk limits.

Check: Every client has a prioritized objective, a fact-based constraint map, and a visible list of unresolved questions.

Step 5: Challenge the first view, then verify material claims

Decision Record: ANALYST DECISION LOG · AI AUDIT LOG when used

As a committee, ask what reasonable alternative interpretation could overturn each role's starting judgment. Record the strongest alternative rejected and the key trade-off in log columns I–J. You may use AI to surface counterarguments or missing risks after preserving your own view. Use only information approved for that tool; do not paste student information, credentials, or licensed FactSet material into a public AI service. AI cannot supply missing client facts.

Optional challenge prompt
We have a provisional client-mandate judgment based on a fictional, shareable summary: [generalized goal and constraint]. What is the strongest alternative reading? Which assumptions, risk-capacity versus risk-willingness conflicts, and information gaps should our committee investigate? Give questions and possible failure conditions, not a client recommendation, invented fact, or citation.

Check each material fact or number against the assigned case file, team workbook, or approved dated source. A reasonable assumption cannot be verified as a client fact; label its basis and what later confirmation could change. In the log, record the claim type, source or exact file location, as-of date when applicable, verification result, change to reasoning, final judgment, guardrail, and peer reviewer (columns H and K–S). Summarize material AI use in AI AUDIT LOG. If a key claim is contradicted or unsupported, revise it or carry it as an explicit open issue.

Check: The log shows how human judgment changed or held up after challenge and source review; AI output is never cited as evidence.

Step 6: Translate the submitted macro view into three guardrails

Submitted Part 1 forecast · Decision Record · IPS template

For each client, explain one or two implications of the submitted 12-month base and bear cases for that client's goals. Specify a defensible return target, maximum annualized portfolio volatility, and an explicit drawdown tripwire that would force review. State the measure, horizon, and reason for each threshold. Distinguish the client-data standard deviation from the committee's chosen maximum. Add testable downstream rules for liquidity, income, concentration, values, and other relevant constraints.

If the committee changes the market view after Part 1, identify the original submitted view, the new evidence, and the reason in the Decision Record and mandate memo. Do not silently replace the submitted macro file.

Check: Three different clients have three justified mandates; each limit can be checked later against a proposed portfolio.

Step 7: Complete one IPS document per client

IPS template: Sections 1–6

Make three copies of the IPS template. Complete its six short sections: goals and supplied facts; return and risk mandate; material constraints; provisional allocation; implementation and review policy; and open issues and committee approval. Missing client facts may remain “Not provided.” Keep answers concise and cite the assigned slide or data row for material facts.

Assess the supplied target return and standard deviation, then justify the committee's annual return objective, volatility limit, and drawdown tripwire. Recommend broad allocation weights totaling 100% and a practical review rule. These are proposed investment policies, not invented client facts. Do not equate net worth with investable assets or infer an unknown cash requirement. Specific securities and final optimized weights belong to Phase 2. No client interviews are required; no client signature is needed.

Check: Each IPS stands on its own, matches its evidence and Decision Log, and clearly distinguishes approved policy from later portfolio choices.

Step 8: Write the one-page committee mandate memo

Committee memo · one page, all three clients

Summarize the submitted macro view and any documented revision, then give each client a compact mandate: priority goal, material constraint, return target, volatility limit, drawdown tripwire, and one material assumption, information gap, or trade-off. State how the same macro outlook leads to different client decisions. Cite the submitted workbook and specific client evidence or Decision Log entries where a reader can trace the reasoning.

Check: A committee member can understand and compare all three mandates on one page without opening every supporting file.

Committee approval and hand-in

Complete Gate 1, then submit the client package

Step 9: Peer-review the log and hold Gate 1

Decision Record: ANALYST DECISION LOG · PHASE 1

Before voting, check the log's 15-cell role-by-client coverage table and “READY FOR PHASE 1 GATE” status. Have a second member review each entry's evidence and final guardrail. In PHASE 1, list the evidence reviewed, write a motion covering the shared market view and all three client mandates, and record each distinct member's vote, evidence, initials, and any reservation or condition. A dual-role member votes once.

All four or five members must vote Approve for the sheet to show APPROVED. Any Revise or Reject vote requires an action owner, deadline, completed revision evidence, and a new meeting; do not treat an unresolved revision as approval. Record dissent and conditions even when the motion passes. Portfolio construction starts only after Gate 1 approval.

Check: The Decision Record preserves the vote and action trail, and the three IPS mandates match what the committee approved.
