Before using AI

Form and preserve your own interpretation

Step 1: Start the Macro Starter workbook

Macro Starter: Team Information · Economic Outlook Roles

Open Macro_Starter_Template_Student.xlsx. On Team Information, enter the team name and four or five distinct members. Assign all five official committee roles, then save a team copy as BUS331_[TeamName]_Phase1_Macro.xlsx. On a four-person team, one member holds two roles; that person still counts as one member and later casts one vote. Use Economic Outlook Roles to divide the macro research and peer review.

Check: Each role has an owner, and every member knows which evidence they will bring to the team.

Step 2: Read the historical record before forecasting

Workbook: Historical Data

Review the five-year window and the latest observations for real GDP, CPI inflation, the 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread, consumer sentiment, and the effective fed funds rate. Note each source, observation period, unit, and retrieval date. GDP is quarterly; the other series are monthly. GDP quarter-over-quarter annualized growth differs from four-quarter year-over-year growth. The yield spread is in percentage points, not a percentage return; a negative spread means the 2-year yield exceeds the 10-year yield.

Keep historical observations separate from forecasts. Leave missing observations blank rather than estimating them. A negative yield spread is a signal to investigate, not a dated recession prediction.

Check: You can explain what each measure says, what period it describes, and one important limit.

Step 3: Write the human evidence read

Workbook: Phase 1 Worksheet, columns B–E

Answer the worksheet questions yourselves before opening an AI tool. For each response, name the exact series and period, give the source link or publication and retrieval date, and explain why the observation matters or where it is uncertain. Compare signals that disagree; do not turn a correlation into a causal claim.

Useful writing pattern

“We observed [measure and period]. We interpret it as [possible meaning] because [supporting evidence]. An alternative reading is [other explanation]. We would reconsider if [observable signal].”

Check: Someone outside your team can tell which sentences are observations and which are your interpretations.

Step 4: Record an initial committee view

Workbook: Initial Estimate vs AI, column B

Discuss the evidence as a team. Enter your initial view for the economic regime, GDP growth, inflation expectations, fed funds path, sentiment, and primary risk or yield curve. State a 12-month direction or estimate when you can defend one, plus a reason and a key uncertainty. Preserve column B as the pre-AI view, even if later evidence changes your conclusion.

Check: Every topic has a human-authored view before any AI challenge is recorded.

Step 5: Put numbers and comparisons on the same basis

Workbook: Numerical Expectations

Read the latest observed value and period in columns B–C. Where available, enter a source-dated, genuinely comparable external forecast or consensus in D–G. Enter the team's initial 12-month base estimate in H, with rationale and reviewer or uncertainty in I–J. Copy the initial estimate, measure, and horizon into the matching human-view entry on Initial Estimate vs AI before using AI; a later change to H must not erase the starting number. Include a CPI inflation expectation. If no comparable external forecast exists for a measure, leave its comparison cell blank and explain that in the rationale.

Check each comparison's definition, units, and horizon. A historical FRED observation is not a forecast or consensus, and a different inflation measure or date is not automatically comparable.

Check: Your initial numerical view is recorded before AI, and every external number has a publication or as-of date.

After the initial view is saved

Use AI to challenge; verify the result yourselves

Step 6: Ask for the strongest challenge to your thesis

Workbook: Initial Estimate vs AI, column C

Give the tool a short summary of your human view, the public observations you used, their periods, and your stated uncertainties. Ask it to find competing interpretations, weak assumptions, missing indicators, unit or horizon mismatches, and observations that would disconfirm your base case. Ask for questions to investigate, not a finished forecast.

Prompt you can adapt
Our team’s human-written 12-month macro view is: [paste your own summary]. We based it on these public, dated observations: [series, value, unit, period]. Our main uncertainty is [uncertainty]. Act as a skeptical committee member. Give the strongest plausible alternative interpretation, identify assumptions or measure mismatches we may have missed, and name observable evidence that would change our view. Distinguish facts from hypotheses. Do not invent data, citations, or a final answer for us.

Use only public or otherwise approved, shareable information in an AI tool. Do not paste client profiles, student information, credentials, proprietary FactSet captures, or licensed exports into it.

Check: The AI output is framed as a challenge, not as a source or an answer to copy.

Step 7: Investigate each material challenge and decide

Workbook: Initial Estimate vs AI, columns C–H

Summarize the useful AI challenge in C and isolate any claim or number that needs checking in D. Verify it in FRED, the original release, an approved licensed source, or another credible primary source; record the source and as-of period or date in E. In F, mark the claim accepted, rejected, or uncertain. In G, explain the team's final view and why it changed or stayed the same. Have another member review it in H.

If the challenge improves your forecast, update the final estimate and rationale on Numerical Expectations while keeping the original column B view intact. If a claim cannot be verified, leave it out of the final reasoning or label the uncertainty clearly. AI output and AI-suggested citations are never independent evidence.

Check: A reader can trace each material revision from the human starting point through the challenge, source check, and final decision.

Committee conclusion

Translate the final view into scenarios and submit

Step 8: Build distinct bull, base, and bear cases

Workbook: Bull Base Bear Cases · Risk Matrix

Use the same 12-month horizon and metric definitions in all three cases. The Base case links to the final team forecasts in Numerical Expectations H5–H8. Enter the bull and bear estimates, a probability for every case, distinct drivers, dated evidence, client or asset implications, and a signal that would challenge each case. Probabilities must total 100%; the weighted result is meaningful only when all case inputs are complete.

In Risk Matrix, explain how each current signal could fit each case. Add a watch threshold, a possible client or asset response, and an owner or review date. A threshold tells the team when to reconsider; it is not proof that one case will occur.

Check: Inflation appears in all cases, the probability check passes, and the risk responses follow from the evidence rather than an AI assertion.
